PipeCrafter 3D Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 15, 2026
Last updated: November 15, 2026

PipeCrafter 3D (the "App") is a design app, provided by H Labs Works (the "Operator"), for combining pipes and joints in 3D space and checking their dimensions and parts lists. This page explains what information the App handles and how, following what the App actually does.

1. Operator and contact

  • Operator: H Labs Works
  • Contact: info@hlabsworks.com

2. Information the Operator does not collect

  • The App has no sign-up and no login. You never enter your name, e-mail address or phone number.
  • The Operator does not run a server of its own for receiving users' information. Delivering the list of purchase links (section 7) and receiving crash reports (section 5) are left to external services. The designs (projects) you make are never sent to a server of the Operator.
  • The App does not ask for access to the camera, photos, location, contacts or microphone.
  • The App's own code does not read any advertising identifier (the iOS IDFA, the Android advertising ID). The Google component that shows ads (section 4) handles them.

3. Information stored on your device

The following is stored on your device and does not reach the Operator (for backups, see "Backups" below; for what you share or export yourself, see section 8).

  • Designs: saved projects, autosaved drafts, and the small preview pictures (thumbnails) shown in the list.
  • Settings: units, default colour, text size, the way 3D is drawn, the crash-report setting, progress through the first-run guide and so on.
  • Points record: the balance of points earned by watching rewarded videos, and the key and other information that protect that record. It is stored encrypted; the key is kept in the iOS Keychain or, on Android, in a preferences file protected by the Android Keystore. The points record contains nothing that identifies you.
  • A copy of the list of purchase links (section 7).
  • What the advertising and consent component (Google) remembers, such as your consent choice, and what the crash-report component (Firebase) assigns, such as an installation identifier. These may be stored on the device by those components themselves; where and what is stored follows Google's own description.
  • Temporary files made for sharing. They are removed when sharing ends, or the next time the App starts.

Backups: on iOS, the device backup (including iCloud Backup) may include the App's data, designs among it. On Android, Auto Backup and a transfer to another device may include designs, but the points record and its key are excluded.

How to delete:

  • A design: on the home screen, choose "Delete" from the project's menu. Both the saved content and the autosaved content are removed and cannot be restored.
  • Everything: deleting the App from your device removes the designs and settings. On iOS, however, part of the points record (a key and a copy of the balance) may stay in the Keychain. This lets the points carry over when you reinstall the App on the same device; it does not identify you.
  • What was included in a backup is removed through the backup itself (iCloud or Google backup).

4. Advertising (Google AdMob)

In the free version, the App shows ads in a strip at the top of the screen (home and editor), and you can earn points by watching a rewarded video. The App uses Google's advertising services (Google AdMob and the Google Mobile Ads SDK) to serve and measure ads. While you hold a Pro subscription, no ads are shown.

Each time the free version starts, before any ad is shown, it asks Google whether consent is needed. This request, too, necessarily delivers your IP address and similar information to Google. In regions where consent is not needed, ads start without asking for consent.

When ads are shown, Google's component may automatically collect the following for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and share it with Google and advertising partners.

  • IP address (which may be used to estimate the approximate location of the device)
  • Advertising identifiers (the iOS IDFA, the Android advertising ID and similar) and information that identifies the device or the app
  • Records of interactions such as ads shown, taps and video views
  • Diagnostics and performance information such as start-up time, hang rate and power use, and records of faults in the ad component itself (crash logs; iOS)

The details of what is collected follow Google's own description. The Operator does not receive these individual items of information (what the Operator receives is aggregate reporting from AdMob, such as impressions and revenue). For how Google handles it, see Google's Privacy Policy.

Consent and permission:

  • In regions where the law requires consent (such as the EEA), Google's consent screen (its consent-management mechanism) is shown before any ad is shown.
  • On iOS, Apple's "tracking" permission dialog may follow Google's consent screen. If you do not allow it, ads are still shown and you earn points in the same way. The dialog reads: "Allowing this lets us show ads more relevant to you. If you don't, you'll still see ads and earn points the same way."
  • Where consent is required and cannot be confirmed, no ads are shown and rewarded videos cannot be watched.

To change your choice later:

  • In the app settings, "Ad privacy settings" reopens Google's consent choices. This row appears only in regions where Google requires the entry (such as the EEA).
  • iOS: in "Settings", "Privacy & Security", "Tracking", you can change the permission for the App.
  • Android: in the Android settings you can reset or delete the advertising ID.

5. Crash reports

When the App stops unexpectedly, it sends information about that to the Operator through Google's Firebase Crashlytics. You can turn this off at any time with "Send crash reports" in the app settings (it is on by default). Once it is off, nothing is sent after that.

What is sent is mainly the following: a record of what the App was doing when it stopped (a stack trace), the state of the App at that moment, the type of device and OS, a record of the warnings and errors just before, the per-installation identifier that Crashlytics assigns (used to count how many installations a crash affects), the Firebase installation identifier (Google states that it does not uniquely identify you or your device), and the Firebase user agent (a string showing the device, OS, app identifier and development platform). On Android it also includes session information such as the app's package name, the OS, the type of network connection, the device's manufacturer and model, and the times the app came to the foreground; on iOS similar information may be included (the details follow Google's own description). It does not include your name, a user ID, the name or content of your projects, or file locations.

6. In-app purchases (the Pro plan)

The Pro subscription is processed by Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play). The Operator does not receive your card number, Apple ID or Google account details. At each start the App asks the store whether you hold Pro, only to decide what to show (ads and so on); it does not store the answer on your device and does not send it to the Operator. You manage or cancel the subscription on the store's page.

The Operator may receive aggregate sales information that the stores provide to developers (such as the number of sales).

7. Purchase links and affiliate programmes

From "Where to buy" in the parts list you can open links to shops' search pages or product pages. Opening a link takes you out of the App (to the browser or the shop's app). What happens to information after that follows the policy of that shop or of the service that brokers the link, and cookies and the like may be used in the browser. The App does not add any identifier of yours to a link.

To keep the list of purchase links up to date, the App may fetch one configuration file published by the Operator from a delivery service on the internet (GitHub Pages). In a version where this fetch is enabled, each fetch necessarily delivers your IP address and a User-Agent that shows the type and version of the networking library to the host. The App sends no cookies or identifiers, only a value that identifies the version of the file fetched last time. The fetched file is kept on the device and used at the next start.

Advertisement / PR: The shop links may include affiliate links, and the operator of this app may earn a referral fee when you buy.

8. Sharing and export

When you export a CSV, PDF, image or project file, iPhone, iPad and Android open the OS share sheet, and you choose where it goes. What the recipient (an app or service) does with the data follows that recipient's policy. It is not sent to the Operator.

10. Children

The App is not directed at children. In the ad settings it is not treated as child-directed, nor as directed at users under the age of consent. The content of ads is limited to "parental guidance suggested (PG)" and below.

11. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, this page is updated and the "last updated" date is changed.

12. Contact

Please direct questions about this policy or about how the App handles information to the contact in section 1. The main information the Operator receives is the crash reports in section 5, the AdMob aggregate reporting in section 4 and the stores' aggregate sales information in section 6. The Operator also receives whatever you send to the contact address. Once an affiliate programme for the purchase links is active, the Operator may receive reports of results (such as the number of purchases and referral fees) from that programme's operator. We respond in accordance with applicable law.
